What is a composable CDP?

A traditional CDP is one product that does everything. A composable CDP builds the same capability from best-fit parts around the company's own data warehouse or lakehouse. The business keeps control of its data, and each part can be bought or replaced independently.

Your data stays home

Customer data lives in the company's own lakehouse, not copied into a vendor's black box.

Pick the right tool per job

Buy commodity pieces such as connectors to ad platforms. Build the pieces that give real advantage, such as live audience decisions.

Marketers stay in charge

A single audience builder lets marketers define who they want to reach, without writing code or joins.

The architecture

Data flows top to bottom: collected from sources, processed live and in batch, stored in shared stores, defined by a marketer in the audience builder, then delivered to the tools that contact customers.

How an audience gets created

The audience builder walks through these steps. Each one is a section of the interactive prototype.

Describe the audience

Name it, choose how fresh it must be (live per event, scheduled batch, or both), pick the data sources and set how far back to look.

Define who qualifies

Build rules from customer attributes, behaviours and purchase-value scores (Recency, Frequency, Monetary). Combine them with "all of" and "any of" groups.

Add sequences and exits

Describe journeys such as "viewed a product, added it to the cart, but did not check out within 30 minutes". Decide when people leave the audience.

Apply guardrails

Exclude unsubscribed or recent buyers, cap how often a customer is contacted, and require marketing consent.

Choose where it goes

Select destinations such as Braze or ad platforms, and whether they update instantly or on a schedule.

Review before launch

Check validation warnings, an estimated size and sample members. The tool shows the generated definition, the SQL it would produce, and which parts run on bought tools and which on custom-built ones.

Where to buy and where to build

A key decision in a composable CDP is which parts to buy and which to build. This table summarises the reasoning shown in the prototype.

CapabilityIn plain wordsRuns onApproach
Real-time audiencesCustomers join or leave within seconds of an actionStream processingBUILD
Batch audiencesLarge audiences refreshed on a schedule from historyLakehouse SQL + reverse-ETLBUY
Sequences (A then B)Customer did one thing, then another, in order and in timeStream pattern matchingBUILD
Exit rulesRemoving people promptly, for example after they buyStream processingBUILD
Purchase-value scoring (RFM)How recently, how often and how much a customer buysStream + daily snapshotBUY + BUILD
SuppressionNot contacting people who shouldn't be contactedMembership store + activationBUY + BUILD
Sending to channelsKeeping Braze, ad platforms and email up to dateConnectorsBUY

The reasoning: commodity plumbing (connectors, scheduled syncs) is cheaper to buy. Logic that must react in seconds, or that depends on the order of events, is where custom-built stream processing earns its place.

What this prototype shows, and what it does not

What it demonstrates

  • How the layers of a composable CDP fit together
  • How a marketer's choices become a structured definition and an execution plan
  • Real-time, batch and hybrid audiences, and why each exists
  • Trade-offs between buying tools and building components
  • Guardrails that catch risky audience set-ups before launch

What a real build would still need

  • Identity resolution (knowing which records are the same person)
  • Roles, approvals and an audit trail
  • Safe editing and backfill of a live audience
  • Connector set-up, retries and rate limits
  • A consent and preference management interface
  • Cost controls and monitoring for streaming jobs
